# Release notes for pkpdsim 0.18.0

pkpdsim 0.18.0 makes the verdict of a context of use stricter in two ways ([#111](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/pkpdsim/issues/111), [#112](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/pkpdsim/pull/112)). The first models with declarations ([pkdb_models#477](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/pkdb_models/pull/477)) showed two gaps:
- a claimed quantity of interest without decisive data inside the claimed domain;
- a verdict resting on very few points.

A context of use which 0.17.0 found `supported` can now be `insufficient_evidence`.

## Changes

- **Quantity coverage.**
  - Every claimed quantity of interest which is not a ratio must have decisive points inside the claimed domain. Decisive points are evaluable points of the decisive evidence whose metadata has a claimed value in every claimed field.
  - An uncovered quantity counts like an uncovered metadata value: the verdict is at most `supported_with_extrapolation` with an extrapolation text, else `insufficient_evidence`.
  - The coverage and the points of every quantity are in the report, on the release page and in the new `quantities.tsv`. `contexts.tsv` lists the `uncovered_quantities`.
- **A minimum of evaluable points.**
  - A context of use needs at least 5, 10 or 20 evaluable points for a low, medium or high model risk (`DEFAULT_MIN_POINTS`). `ContextOfUse(min_points=...)` overrides the default.
  - Below the minimum the verdict is `insufficient_evidence`. A failing acceptance rule still gives `not_supported`.
  - The minimum is in `contexts.tsv` (`minimum_points`), in the report, on the release page and in the console summary (evaluable/minimum).
- **The verdict order** is now: no decisive point, a rule not met, a rule not evaluable, below the minimum, coverage of the claimed values and quantities, extrapolation. `pkpdsim.credibility.assessment.verdict` takes the keyword arguments `n_evaluable` and `minimum`.
- **Records of 0.17.0** still read with `run_credibility --from-json`. Their minimum is 0 and is not shown; their quantities have no coverage.
- **Tests.** The CPU limit of the load time test of the PBPK builder is 15 s instead of 10 s; a Windows runner took 10.6 s.
