# Release notes for pkpdsim 0.5.3

pkpdsim 0.5.3 adds a worker cap to the command line of the sensitivity runner and the general form of the state after a change to `pkpdsim.scan`. Two small features found by the port of the models onto sbmlsim 0.9.4; nothing else changes.

## Features

- `SensitivityRunner.main` and `parse_command_line` accept `--workers N`, an int of at least 1; `0` or absent means `n_workers=None` (sbmlsim chooses; the model's own `n_workers` is not used). `CommandLine` gets the field `workers` (`None` if absent or `0`; a third field, so a comparison with a tuple of two items needs the third), and `SensitivityRunner.simulator_settings(workers)` gives the settings of the model with `n_workers=workers` (`None` without workers) for a script with its own `main`, `run(names, simulator=runner.simulator_settings(words.workers))`. The words and `--workers` may come in any order. Why: a pool needs about 300 to 340 MB per process for a PBPK model, so all CPUs of a 20-CPU machine exceed an 8 GB budget, and the batches of the port had to cap the processes with `taskset`.
- `pkpdsim.scan.state_after_changes(res)` gives a result with the state after a change at its time, every time once: `res.interpolate(np.unique(res.time_points()))`, the form of `point_timecourse` for all points of a scan at once, as a `ScanResult`. Why: simvastatin (`states_after_changes`) and chlorzoxazone (`result_on_time_grid`) carry the same one-line helper, which the isolation of the models requires to be a copy and the review of the port named as a candidate for pkpdsim before more models copy it.
